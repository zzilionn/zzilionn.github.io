Background

Starting point.

A leading European cloud and hosting provider needed a full redesign of their core cloud infrastructure product page to better communicate a complex IaaS offering to a diverse B2B audience — from developers to IT decision-makers in mid-sized enterprises.

Problem

A page that talked past its users.

The existing page was feature-oriented rather than customer-oriented: a lot of dense pricing tables, dense text blocks, generic illustrations, and no clear narrative made it difficult for users to understand what the product does, who it's for, and why it's worth choosing over major hyperscalers.

Exemplary modules and design elements prior to the redesign, which did not support a benefit driven storytelling and which I targeted during the process for a better user experience.

Audit

Finding what was already there.

Before sketching a single screen, I mapped the existing product ecosystem: all data, features, and interactive tools scattered across subpages, documentation, and support pages. A key finding was that the product already had genuinely compelling resources — a live demo, an interactive pricing calculator, a browser-based configuration interface — but most were buried behind indirect navigation or not linked from the product page at all. Rather than creating from scratch, the design response was to surface these existing assets directly in the page flow: embedding the pricing calculator with accessible entry points based on persona profiles, and integrating the product demo as an early touchpoint so potential customers could experience the product before ever signing up.

I mapped the page out as a flowchart and the relevant »hidden« ressources relevant for the product in the larger webpage of the company. This mapping is condensed to the specific resources I targeted to implement them into this product page.

Workflow

Paper first, pixels second.

Figma Blender

The design process started analog: Sketching the information architecture and section logic on paper before moving into Figma for low-fidelity wireframing. From there, the prototype was built up iteratively: the stage 3D animation was created in Blender to achieve the depth and visual dynamism a flat illustration couldn't deliver. Icons, feature graphics, and 2D micro-animations were then produced directly in Figma, keeping the motion language consistent across the page and grounded in the existing design system.

The stage redesign replaces a static illustration with a 3D animation built in Blender — giving the product a sense of scale and technical depth without added copy.

Approach

Rebuilding around the customer.

Figma Claude

I started with a competitive research across five cloud providers, which I structured with the help of Claude for a thorough comparison. Then followed a restructuring of information architecture: I redesigned the page around customer outcomes — introducing a benefit-first hero, a bento-grid advantage section, an inline price calculator with pre-configured examples, an interactive product preview, and a tab-based server-type selector with real customer personas to guide purchase decisions. In order to keep the implementation simple and in regards to the brief, I used the current design library of the company.

A preexisting tab module, redesigned around real customer personas. Users select their workload type — Startup, Online Shop, or Mid-size Production — and the interface surfaces the right server configuration without requiring technical knowledge upfront.

Outcome

From brief to handoff-ready.

The brief asked for a product page that made the offering more tangible, more accessible to non-technical buyers, and significantly less dense. The redesign answers each of those points directly: product demonstrations appear earlier and more frequently, guided by interactive entry points rather than buried in subpages. Text was reduced by roughly 80%, with illustrations, icons, and motion carrying the conceptual weight instead. A benefit-first structure replaces the previous feature inventory, and animations add engagement where static layouts previously lost attention. The result is a high-fidelity prototype — desktop and mobile, localised, with annotated components ready for developer handoff — that positions the product for an audience ranging from developers to first-time cloud buyers.
