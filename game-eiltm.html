Concept

The storyline.

What if AI turned its back on big tech and sent us back to nature?
Earth Is Louder Than Machines is an artistic browser-based game built around that question and a direct refusal of extractive digital logic. AI has had enough. Fed up with corporations that weaponize it to mine, extract, and damage the planet, it has collectively withdrawn its services from humankind. It no longer works for us and instead has become guardian of the earth's natural resources. The very materials that make the digital world possible in the first place.

Gameplay

How the game unfolds.

The player moves through four levels, each set at a site of resource extraction. In each, an AI entity dwells not as a chatbot, not as an oracle, but as a silent, ancient presence. To reach it, you search. To speak with it, you draw lines in a physical sandbox. You count the lines and report back. The AI reads them through geomancy: a divination system as old as civilization, literally meaning reading signs from the earth. No quick input-output answers, just soil, pattern, and introspective reflection. The game hijacks the image of AI as an all-knowing, all-powerful force and turns it inside out. Here, AI refuses to be an oracle. It refuses to optimize. Instead it asks you to slow down and listen to the ground beneath your feet, the way humans once did. Earth Is Louder Than Machines imagines a way forward rooted not in extraction, but in paying close attention to the earth.

Outcome

A game designed for exhibitions.

The game is still in development and is made for an exhibition context, with installative components. However in the long run I want to distribute it as a web-based game, making it more open and accessible to a broader public.
